01 / The big idea

So, what is Systems Bioengineering?

Imagine trying to understand a city. Studying only its roads would not explain traffic, energy, hospitals or how people move. You need to understand how everything connects. Living organisms work in a similar way.

Systems Bioengineering is an interdisciplinary approach that combines biological knowledge with engineering principles, quantitative analysis and computational modelling to understand, predict, design and improve the behaviour of complex biological systems.

Rather than looking at a single gene, protein or cell in isolation, it studies how many components interact, how those interactions change over time, and how the whole system responds when something changes. Engineers use this understanding to develop useful, measurable solutions.

Think of a living cell as a city

  • Genes are like instruction manuals.
  • Proteins are like workers and machines.
  • Metabolic pathways are like production lines.
  • Signals are like communication networks.
  • The environment is like the city's available resources and conditions.

Why does the systems perspective matter?

Changing one component can affect several others. A change that improves one process may create a bottleneck elsewhere. Systems thinking helps us understand these interactions instead of optimizing one part blindly.

Key idea: the behaviour of the whole system cannot always be predicted by studying its parts separately.

04 / The engineering mindset

From biological question to engineered solution

Systems bioengineering is not just about building something. It is a continuous process of understanding, modelling, testing and improving.

STEP 01

Understand

Identify the biological problem, the system's components and how they interact.

STEP 02

Model & design

Use data and mathematical models to predict behaviour and propose a solution.

STEP 03

Test

Run experiments, collect measurements and compare real results with predictions.

STEP 04

Improve

Learn from differences, refine the model and redesign the system. Repeat.

This iterative approach matters because biological systems are complex, variable and not always perfectly predictable. A model is a tool for making and testing predictions-not a replacement for experiments.

Medicine & therapeutics

Biomanufacturing

Engineering cells to produce proteins, enzymes, vaccines or useful chemicals.

🌱

Environment & sustainability

Developing biological processes for waste treatment, resource recovery and sustainable production.

These examples involve different levels of biological complexity. The same systems mindset-understand interactions, model behaviour, test and improve-can help connect them.

07 / The takeaway

The whole is more than the sum of its parts.

Systems Bioengineering connects biology with engineering and quantitative thinking. It helps us understand how living components interact, predict how a system may behave, and use that knowledge to design better solutions.

Remember: Understand the system → model its behaviour → test your predictions → improve the design.

That is the central idea: don't just study life-understand how its parts work together, and use that understanding responsibly.
